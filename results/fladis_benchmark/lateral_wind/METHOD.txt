FLADIS lateral variation of the measured mean wind direction

The comparison control is ../mast_wind, which used each trial's station
means as a function of x and averaged the two far-mast wind directions.
This test uses the same four independent meteorological rows in each
SMEDIS workbook. All three cases use one rule. The molecular properties,
source, heat and turbulence closures, time perturbation, particle rate
(120/s), time step (0.25 s), seed, receptor coordinates and averaging
windows are those of the control.

At 70 m and upstream, retain the x-dependent mean direction of the
control. From x=70 to x=227.5 m, linearly increase a transverse gradient
derived from the two actual far-mast positions and measured directions.
Meteorological from-bearings become local slopes through
m = tan(290 degrees - bearing). The far slope is linear in survey y
between the two observed mast values and is extended linearly outside
them. The slopes reproduce the two measured bearings exactly prior
to the inherited temporal direction perturbation.

To compensate transverse spreading in the unperturbed mean field, set
u = U0(z) exp(-integral(B dx)) and v = u (A + B y).
The x-dependent intercept A, including the tiny tangent/circular-mean
offset, and B are derived only from meteorological inputs. Before the
inherited coherent time rotations, du/dx + dv/dy is zero. As in the
preceding test, those rotations and the fixed-frame turbulent covariance
are retained. This is a simplified interpolated field based on time-
averaged 10 m wind observations, not a resolved reconstruction of the
site's time-dependent, height-dependent or building-induced wind.

Verified in an independent input check:
  The interpolated far-mast bearings match all six measured far-mast
  values within 1e-10 deg.
  Finite-difference unrotated horizontal divergence is below 1e-8 s^-1.
  The positive-y slope gradients are about 0.000260, 0.000832 and
  0.001721 m^-1 for Trials 9, 16 and 24.
  The purely kinematic far-field lateral stretch factors are about
  1.023, 1.077 and 1.166. These are diagnostics, not concentration fits.

The paired-sensor plume center is the concentration-weighted y centroid.
The model's arc scan width and peak are compared to the published SMEDIS
summaries. Paired-sensor width and peak are also retained. Nominal 240 m
includes the actual 238/240 m arcs and surveyed receptor coordinates.
FLACS comparison uses the public Gexcon CERC-source Pasquill D fixed-
height arc maximum, with its C/E envelope in the CSV. Spatial center
and width outputs are absent from that submission.
